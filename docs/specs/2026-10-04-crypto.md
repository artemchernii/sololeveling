## What gets built

1. **The Revolut crypto statement is read.** It goes through the trade
   reader that already exists (a short list, reviewed and then
   confirmed). New in what the reader returns:
   - per trade: `fee`, and `crypto: true` for a coin
   - side `reward` for a staking payment, at price 0
   - the account summary's **closing amount per coin**
2. **What gets written on confirm**, into the account he picks (his one
   Revolut):
   - each buy, sell and staking payment, on its own day
   - dollar trades turned into € at **the rate of their day**, not today's
   - buys of coins counted net of the fee Revolut takes in coins
   - staking at €0 cost (`reward`), never income
   - crypto trades never move Revolut's cash (the bank statement already
     has that money)
   - the closing amounts written as what the statement showed on its
     date (`holdings`), so each coin says "statement and trades agree"
     or by how much they differ — the same check his shares have
3. **Rates back to 2020.** When a file's trades are older than the stored
   rates, the reader fetches that currency's daily ECB rates (Frankfurter)
   back to the first trade and stores them.
4. **Coins found as coins.** A coin's ticker is `SYM-EUR` on Yahoo, priced
   by the nightly job like any position.
5. **Portfolio shows four kinds** — stocks, ETFs, gold, crypto — in a split
   under WORTH NOW; a coin row says "+4.91 free from staking"; "you paid
   for them" becomes "bought for".
6. **Logos by what a thing is:** a coin's own icon; an ETF's issuer
   (iShares, Vanguard, …) by its name; a company by its ticker as today.
