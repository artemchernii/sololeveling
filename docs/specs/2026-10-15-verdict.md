## Screen

The built page, unchanged except:

1. **VERDICT panel** at the top of SIGNALS (above the signal rows):
   - caps `VERDICT · 15 OCT 2026` (the day of the latest stored close);
   - the badge `ADD` (good, plus icon) / `HOLD` (lavender, pause icon) /
     `WAIT` (warn, hourglass icon), large, pops in and glows once;
   - one plain line: what the textbook says now (one per rule row, below);
   - one fact line: `price $343.86 · avg buy $381.80 · a buy now lowers
the avg buy` (or `raises`) — two stored numbers with their names;
   - reason chips, always all three: `SMA 200 ▲ +9.8%`, `SMA 50 ▼ −4.4%`,
     `RSI 14 — 36`, each coloured by its own row's verdict; the ones that
     made the call wear the verdict's ring;
   - feeds row: `TECHNICAL · 3 LINES` lit; `FUNDAMENTALS · NEXT` and
     `NEWS · NEXT` dashed, so he sees where they plug in;
   - `RULE ⌄` opens the three-row rule table, the row in force lit.
   - No panel when nothing is held (sold out) or SMA 200 has no 200 closes.
2. **WORTH NOW**: euros big, the same in the price's currency under it
   (`$772.31` = shares × latest price), then shares.
3. **P&L** replaces `DOWN SINCE JUN 22`: label `P&L` with an `▲ UP` /
   `▼ DOWN` badge, `−€76.04 −9.9%`, chip `SINCE FIRST BUY · 22 JUN 2026`
   (sold out: `WHEN SOLD · 30 SEP 2026`).
4. **ACCOUNTS**: under each account's worth, its own P&L in colour:
   `▼ −€38.20 · −8.9%` (Revolut), `▼ −€37.84 · −11.2%` (Trade Republic).
   P&L and ACCOUNTS are a bit wider than the first two tiles; on a phone
   they take the whole row.
5. **Motion**: tile numbers and the price count up on open; the verdict
   badge pops and glows once; reasons arrive one by one; gauge dots slide
   from the middle to today's place; a pressed line draws itself from the
   view's left edge to today in 0.7 s.

## The rule (goes in `src/lib/indicators.ts`, `verdict()`)

From the latest stored close `p`, `sma(50)`, `sma(200)`, `rsi(14)`;
checked top to bottom, first match wins.

| Verdict | When                        | Line on screen                                                                                                                                |
| ------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| WAIT    | RSI 14 > 70                 | RSI 14 above 70: the price rose too fast in 14 days. By the textbook, a pullback often comes next.                                            |
| WAIT    | p < SMA 50 and p < SMA 200  | Price below its 50- and 200-day averages: the trend is down. By the textbook, no new shares until the price is back above the 50-day average. |
| ADD     | p > SMA 200 and p < SMA 50  | Long trend up, and the price dipped under its 50-day average. By the textbook, a dip in an uptrend is a time to add shares.                   |
| ADD     | p > SMA 200 and RSI 14 < 45 | Long trend up, and the price fell fast in 14 days. By the textbook, a dip in an uptrend is a time to add shares.                              |
| HOLD    | every other case            | Price above its 50- and 200-day averages: the trend is up, with no dip. By the textbook, keep the shares; no rush to add at this price.       |

(HOLD's other case, p < SMA 200 and p > SMA 50, gets its own line:
"Price above its 50-day average and below its 200-day average: the
trend is turning. By the textbook, keep the shares and watch SMA 200.")

The average buy is a fact beside the verdict, never an input to it.
Fundamentals and news join the rule in their own slices, each with its
row written here first.

## Data (CLAUDE.md four sources)

- Verdict and chips: `signals()` inputs, from stored closes only
  (the exception of 13/15 Oct). Nothing stored.
- `$772.31`: shares (state) × latest stored price (external reading) —
  the same product the list already shows in euros, in the price's own
  currency.
- P&L per account: the round's buys and sells on that account (log sum,
  `rounds()` by `accountId`) against that account's worth now. The sum of
  the accounts equals the tile.

verdict or history of verdicts.
